# Round runtime boundary

Owner: domain engineers. Public API: `@daisy/debate-engine`. The engine is framework-free and has no ambient clock, I/O, or persistence handle.

Every competitive occurrence is one Round, whether its seats hold people or bots and whether it is ranked or casual, the one unranked type ([ADR 0058](../decisions/0058-one-round-model.md)). A durable Room resolves a `FormatDefinition` and `RoomConfig` into a `RoomExecutionPlan` and fully resolved `RoundRules`. One pure selection policy, `decideFormatSelection`, decides every catalog choice from facts the adapter reads under lock for the exact (format, revision, preset) tuple ([ADR 0078](../decisions/0078-standard-formats-league-templates.md)): a casual room takes a listed format at its canonical preset, adopts that preset's format settings as its config (a Room config embeds format settings and adds Room-only inputs, ADR 0078 §10), and records it as the room's basis, ranked play takes a ranked-eligible preset, Room setup's `select-format` chooses another, and Back to standard restores the exact pinned basis. Only a custom room supplies its own definition and config. `formatStanding` derives, never stores, whether a room still runs its standard: equal compiled rules and room plan to an unchanged standard basis, or Custom with a typed list of changes. The Room pins its format and preset revisions, executes any pre-round prep, and freezes its seats and rules into a scheduled Round. `startRound()` never compiles rules.

PostgreSQL owns the Round lifecycle, participant seats, command log, segment intervals, utterances, ballots, and rating ledger. The one open `round_segments` row is the live interval. A Round's checkpoint holds only state with no segment row: side-keyed in-round prep consumption, the one prep state (idle, called ahead, running or in its microphone lead), and the one point-of-information state (none, offered or being given) ([ADR 0936](../decisions/0936-points-of-information-and-one-prep-state.md)). The engine is one pure function, `stepRound(state, rules, event)` ([ADR 0911](../decisions/0911-round-engine-step-function.md)): from the Round's state (its lifecycle, checkpoint, segment rows and cast), its frozen rules and one event (a command, a tick, activation, a stop, an adjudication, panel completion, a start-gate observation or a platform incident) it returns the next state, the clock changes and the effects, all as data. `projectRoundStep` turns a step into the durable writes and is the only place ids are minted. `createRoundRuntime` hydrates the state from those rows, steps and projects it for each call, and hands the projection to the adapter to persist under a version check. A refusal leaves the runtime unchanged. The runtime itself is disposable process-local execution state, never a durable session.

Every floor command names its segment (`sequence`); a press naming another is refused as stale, so a late End never closes the next speech ([ADR 0936](../decisions/0936-points-of-information-and-one-prep-state.md)). Only the scheduled speaker ends a speech early, crediting unused time back to prep when the rules return time. Cross-examination keeps every seated debater's microphone open (ADR 0096) and takes no points of information; its asker may always end it early, a person answering a bot asker may too, and ending it returns no time to prep. Prep may be called during the segment before the one it is for and starts at that segment's close instant; either partner on the side calls, starts or ends it, and bots take none. A one-speaker speech takes points of information when its rules allow them: a seat of another side offers outside the protected start and end, the speaker accepts one or declines, and the point ends when its holder or the speaker ends it, or by itself at its limit. Offer lapse and a point's end are clock events stamped at their exact instants; the speech clock runs throughout.

Seats are authoritative `round_participants` rows with surrogate participant ids. They are not copied into a snapshot. The runtime verifies each row against the resolved rules and refuses a Round with missing or duplicate required slots. The three identities have different lifetimes: `actors.id` identifies the person or bot, `round_participants.id` identifies that actor's seat in one Round, and `agent_runs.id` identifies one AI execution on a seat. A judge uses the same seat model and the shared ballot contract.

The application passes an explicit actor or service principal and one database-sourced UTC instant into execution. Time changes are projected as segment closes and inserts, then written in that order so the partial unique index can enforce at most one open segment. The final segment remains open until the ballot completes the Round. Command IDs and SHA3-256 payload digests are stored with the resulting version for idempotency. Callers rehydrate after a version conflict instead of replaying stale state.

The browser may derive a read-only virtual position through `@daisy/debate-engine/position`; this pure entrypoint works under the nonce CSP. It never writes the virtual rows back. The server persists actual clock movement through the runtime. Tests cover the same countdown, prep, live segment, and ballot boundary on both paths.

Legal Round commands are derived once, in `round-command-legality.ts`. `applyRoundCommand` raises that function's refusal, and the browser reads `roundCommandOptionsAt` from the same entrypoint, adding only words. The engine's contract walks fail whenever it refuses an option the browser offered as legal.

Judging starts from the server, never the browser. Round maintenance freezes the transcript once, after the final segment and its admission cutoff. Human judges rule against that frozen revision. AI judges run as leased server jobs and only on a complete freeze (DEC-208), so no judge decides on generated words that were never heard ([rooms.md](rooms.md#judging)).

Ratings are pure functions beside the runtime ([ADR 0055](../decisions/0055-glicko-2-calculation-ladders-and-seasons.md)). `ratePeriod` implements Glicko-2; `ratingEligibility` and `planRating` decide whether a completed Round updates the Ranked ladder and produce the two ledger changes. The adapter loads the stored facts and writes the plan transactionally. Ratings never come from Redis or the Round's state.

A debater's record is a pure function too (ISSUE-600). `recordResult` decides what one Round means to one seat, and `tallyRecord` sums the adapter's grouped seats into wins and losses. Only a completed Round and only a debating seat count: the outcome names the winning side (a win for that side, a loss for the other). A forfeit completes the Round with the other side's outcome, so the side that stayed wins and the forfeiter loses. A completed Round with no outcome (no judges, judges past the deadline, or an incomplete transcript) is `no-ruling`: it is listed in match history, but it is never counted, and never as a loss. Abandoned, scheduled and live Rounds and judge seats are not part of the record. The record is computed from `rounds` and `round_participants` on every read; there is no stored counter to drift or replay. It is unrated: no rating appears beside it.

Run `bun test packages/debate-engine/src` for deterministic runtime and rating tests. Service-backed persistence proof lives in `packages/db/integration/` and runs with `bun test:integration`.

The resolution draw is part of a Round's opening ([ADR 0104](../decisions/0104-round-owns-its-opening.md)). `drawResolutions`, `settleDraw` and `preRoundPhase` are pure: the seed and salt come from the OS CSPRNG at the web edge, strikes carry their own due instants, and the Room projection delivers drawn texts only to seated debaters.

A platform incident holds the Round's clock through one checkpoint state and a table of pure transitions; no player reaches them. See [platform incident](../architecture/round-platform-incident.md).
