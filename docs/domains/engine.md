# Round runtime boundary

Owner: domain engineers. Public API: `@daisy/debate-engine`; the Adobe ECS adapter is internal. The runtime is framework-free and has no ambient clock, I/O, or persistence handle.

Every competitive occurrence is one Round, whether its seats hold people or bots and whether it is ranked or casual, the one unranked type ([ADR 0058](../decisions/0058-one-round-model.md)). A durable Room resolves a `FormatDefinition` and `RoomConfig` into a `RoomExecutionPlan` and fully resolved `RoundRules`. One pure selection policy, `decideFormatSelection`, decides every catalog choice from facts the adapter reads under lock for the exact (format, revision, preset) tuple ([ADR 0078](../decisions/0078-standard-formats-league-templates.md)): a casual room takes a listed format at its canonical preset, adopts that preset's format settings as its config (a Room config embeds format settings and adds Room-only inputs, ADR 0078 §10), and records it as the room's basis, ranked play takes a ranked-eligible preset, Room setup's `select-format` chooses another, and Back to standard restores the exact pinned basis. Only a private custom room supplies its own definition and config. `formatStanding` derives, never stores, whether a room still runs its standard: equal compiled rules and room plan to an unchanged standard basis, or Custom with a typed list of changes. The Room pins its format and preset revisions, executes any pre-round prep, and freezes its seats and rules into a scheduled Round. `startRound()` never compiles rules.

PostgreSQL owns the Round lifecycle, participant seats, command log, segment intervals, utterances, ballots, and rating ledger. The one open `round_segments` row is the live interval. A Round's checkpoint holds only state with no segment row: side-keyed in-round prep consumption and anchor, plus the floor after an accepted interruption. The runtime hydrates from those rows and the frozen rules, executes one legal command or clock tick in an isolated ECS store, and returns a projection for the adapter to persist under a version check. A refusal leaves the runtime unchanged. The runtime itself is disposable process-local execution state, never a durable session.

Only the floor holder may yield (DEC-203). An interrupter's yield clears the floor and returns it to the segment's scheduled seat; that segment and its clock continue. In cross-examination the scheduled seat is the questioner, so a respondent who interrupted hands the floor back to them. Only the scheduled speaker's yield closes the segment early, crediting unused time back to prep when the rules return time.

Seats are authoritative `round_participants` rows with surrogate participant ids. They are not copied into a snapshot. The runtime verifies each row against the resolved rules and refuses a Round with missing or duplicate required slots. The three identities have different lifetimes: `actors.id` identifies the person or bot, `round_participants.id` identifies that actor's seat in one Round, and `agent_runs.id` identifies one AI execution on a seat. A judge uses the same seat model and the shared ballot contract.

The application passes an explicit actor or service principal and one database-sourced UTC instant into execution. Time changes are projected as segment closes and inserts, then written in that order so the partial unique index can enforce at most one open segment. The final segment remains open until the ballot completes the Round. Command IDs and SHA3-256 payload digests are stored with the resulting version for idempotency. Callers rehydrate after a version conflict instead of replaying stale state.

The browser may derive a read-only virtual position through `@daisy/debate-engine/position`; this pure entrypoint loads no ECS codegen and works under the nonce CSP. It never writes the virtual rows back. The server persists actual clock movement through the runtime. Tests cover the same countdown, prep, live segment, and ballot boundary on both paths.

Judging starts from the server, never the browser. Round maintenance freezes the transcript once, after the final segment and its admission cutoff. Human judges rule against that frozen revision. AI judges run as leased server jobs and only on a complete freeze (DEC-208), so no judge decides on generated words that were never heard ([rooms.md](rooms.md#judging)).

Ratings are pure functions beside the runtime ([ADR 0055](../decisions/0055-glicko-2-calculation-ladders-and-seasons.md)). `ratePeriod` implements Glicko-2; `ratingEligibility` and `planRating` decide whether a completed Round updates the Ranked ladder and produce the two ledger changes. The adapter loads the stored facts and writes the plan transactionally. Ratings never come from Redis or the ECS store.

A debater's record is a pure function too (ISSUE-600). `recordResult` decides what one Round means to one seat, and `tallyRecord` sums the adapter's grouped seats into wins, losses and draws. Only a completed Round and only a debating seat count: the outcome names the winning side (a win for that side, a loss for the other) or a draw. A forfeit completes the Round with the other side's outcome, so the side that stayed wins and the forfeiter loses. A completed Round with no outcome (no judges, judges past the deadline, or an incomplete transcript) is `no-ruling`: it is listed in match history, but it is never counted, and never as a loss. Abandoned, scheduled and live Rounds and judge seats are not part of the record. The record is computed from `rounds` and `round_participants` on every read; there is no stored counter to drift or replay. It is unrated: no rating appears beside it.

Run `bun test packages/debate-engine/src` for deterministic runtime and rating tests. Service-backed persistence proof lives in `packages/db/integration/` and runs with `bun test:integration`.

The pre-round resolution draw is a Room phase, not a Round one ([ADR 0076](../decisions/0076-pre-round-resolution-draw.md)). `drawResolutions`, `settleDraw` and `preRoundPhase` are pure: the seed and salt come from the OS CSPRNG at the web edge, strikes carry their own due instants, and the Room projection delivers drawn texts only to seated debaters.
