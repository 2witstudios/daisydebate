# Compliance

Daisy's legal obligations under the GDPR, the UK GDPR, the CCPA (as amended
by the CPRA) and COPPA, and the one module or procedure that meets each.
This page is the source of truth. It is also Daisy's record of processing
(GDPR and UK GDPR Article 30), kept at the level of data classes, not
columns. [ADR 0087](../decisions/0087-compliance-from-obligations.md) is the
decision behind it.

Lawful bases and retention periods marked **proposed** await counsel review
(COMP-4, human sign-off). Nothing on this page is legal advice or an
attestation; it records what the code does and what is still missing.

## Keeping this page true

A change that adds a new data class, a new purpose for an existing class, a
new processor or a new retention period updates this page in the same PR.
Adding a column to an existing class does not: classification happens per
class, and the class's row already covers it. There is no per-column
inventory and no gate; the reviewer checks this page.

## Billing records (BILL-2, BILL-15-r1)

Private Billing economics feeds and reports are operational aggregate projections
under DEC-42's maximum 30-day operational retention, with one bounded
exception: a feed is kept only while its collection window is open (its own
month and the month after, plus a correction run's own month during that run)
and is removed by the next sweep otherwise, whatever its age; a feed for a month
that has not started is never retained. So a feed lives at most until the end of
the month after its own, at most about 62 days from placement. Reports keep the
30-day bound. The point guard approved this bounded
exception, recorded as DEC-372 (an open owner decision). They contain model/source
period counts and financial aggregates, with no account, actor, Round or vendor
customer identifiers; this is data minimization, not an anonymity guarantee.
The dedicated local directory is private mode0700 and regular files are mode0600;
the bounded file adapter refuses oversized, symlinked or over-capacity input.
These projections never replace the separately retained original financial
records or discharge an erasure/refund obligation. No participant route serves
them, and named completeness/margin signals alone enter the existing scrubbed
error reporter. See [Billing economics](billing-economics.md) for the concrete
source, retention and delivery limitations.

Billing stores the account-to-Stripe mapping, local subscription identity,
verified invoice IDs and periods, bounded synchronization receipts, and the
account's immutable purchase offer/admission, possible execution and minimal
Checkout identity. It does
not store card details, invoice/customer contact payloads, webhook bodies or
vendor error messages. The proposed lawful basis is performance of the paid
service contract. These records are private and included in the subject's
account export; internal claim identifiers and discovery cursors are excluded.

Subscription facts last for the account's life. Local closure removes them
and all grants in the same canonical account transaction. Only the minimal
customer/unfinished-purchase references needed to cancel, resolve an in-flight
charge and obtain vendor deletion confirmation remain as an unpaid erasure
obligation. Elapsed time or a vendor outage cannot falsely complete it. Once
confirmed, the identifying mapping is removed. Webhook receipt retention is
proposed at 30 days; receipt replay still requires current vendor evidence and
current account authority. Stripe's own financial-record obligations and live
processor terms require the BILL-H owner/counsel activation review.

Local erasure removes undispatched and positively expired purchase records;
possibly executed or completed purchases remain only for outstanding vendor
cleanup. Local closure excludes every ordinary member admission and completion
write. It does not by itself claim cancellation, refund or vendor deletion.
Late verified Session facts retain a closed cleanup mapping and atomically
replace the existing Stripe privacy-job identity. Identical facts do not reset
the job. A new verified event for that closed mapping retains only its minimal
receipt identity to deduplicate delivery and reopens cleanup; no event body is
stored. Old job completion cannot acknowledge a replacement obligation. Refund
identity and original-period accounting must survive job rotation, and unknown
purchase/payment/refund outcomes remain owed until positively resolved.
Minimal refund-action routing is a separate private financial-cleanup record:
original account, mode, customer/charge/refund/invoice/subscription-period,
amount and verified outcome chronology, without contact/payment-method payloads.
It must survive ordinary customer deletion and an earlier cleanup success so
a later failed or action-required refund can reopen the same obligation. There
is no automatic 30-day purge or assumption that elapsed time proves finality.
Production lawful retention duration and erasure guarantees remain pending
owner/counsel review at BILL-H; this is not a new claim of complete erasure.

The required pre-refund observations are a distinct minimal private financial
cleanup record: actual closed owner, mode, customer, invoice and known
InvoicePayment identity, frozen source reference, positively verified facts and
explicit unresolved/classified/original-refund linkage. An invoice enumeration
witness uses the actual invoice ID when no payment ID is known. No charge,
amount, chronology or eligibility is invented. These records hold no worker
claim, card/contact payload or raw vendor response. Subject export includes the
retained financial facts and omits execution authenticators/cursors. Unresolved
observations and necessary routing survive closure, ordinary customer deletion,
job rotation and vendor disappearance; only positive classification or valid
original-action linkage changes their obligation. No elapsed finality purge or
lawful-duration decision is introduced. The Billing adopter exports these facts
and retains their routing through closure. Atomic persist-before-cursor delivery
and fair revisits remain implementation work under BILL15r1.

An exact owned payment may retain verified paid status and original second
precision as a minimal pre-closure classification, without charge/allocation or
period details. Unknown fields remain unknown. This evidence resolves only that
payment; conflicting evidence remains owed and an existing original refund-action
binding survives independently. Observation identifiers/source/known financial
facts are non-erasing; the runtime role has no ordinary DELETE privilege. This
does not settle the pending production lawful-duration/erasure decision.

The privacy adopter and synchronization implementation are in progress on the
Billing slice. Vendor cleanup delivery and receipt retention must be wired and
proved before enabling the production billing writer; this section is not a
claim that those pending paths already operate.

## Release-control targeting and audit (FLAGS)

Allowance accounting records the account's own Round request, source and period,
first dispatch, provider outcome, and once-only refund or platform-credit reason.
It contains no transcript or payment credentials. These private records support
contract delivery and quota enforcement during the account's life, export through
`usage-privacy.ts`, and are deleted on account closure under the canonical account
fence. DEC-316 forbids retaining a cross-account identity or linking a later signup.
A late provider result cannot recreate allowance or dispatch authority. Provider
execution evidence remains governed by its existing purpose, separately from quota
restoration.

Release controls are not entitlements or authorization. `flag_account_overrides`
contains account-linked rollout selection (account id, key, enabled, creation and
expiry). Its purpose is controlled release testing; the proposed lawful basis is
legitimate interests. It is inactive at its expiry even if maintenance stops.
The bounded retention sweep removes it 24 hours after expiry (at most 500 rows per
batch, 20 batches per pass); account erasure removes it immediately under the
canonical account fence. The subject export includes only that subject's rows.

`flag_change_events` holds key, revision, command id, change kind and time, without
an account id or targeting values. It is linked through the shared
`operator_command_receipts`: operator/service references, optional target account,
request digest, result and admin audit head are personal or linkable security
evidence, **not anonymous because they are hashed**. Their purpose is change
accountability and safe command retries; the proposed lawful basis is legitimate
interests. Flags evidence has a **proposed 90-day retention period**, pruned in
bounded batches by the existing maintenance sweep. No audit payload retains
arbitrary operator-supplied notes or the override values. Subject export includes
its command id, action, result and timestamp; internal operator identifiers and
digests are excluded.

Normal runtime writes can append this audit but cannot update or delete it.
Narrow security-definer functions are the only runtime erasure/retention path:
account erasure clears the direct target-account link on Flags receipts atomically,
while keeping command identity, digest and audit events as bounded security evidence;
retention cannot delete evidence newer than 90 days or more than 500 commands per
call. Global state commands have a null subject. Expired receipts may no longer
replay, but every accepted change advances the retained flag revision, so an old
expected revision cannot regain authority. Erased subjects cannot acquire targeting
again, including when targeting races erasure. Erasure does not delete audit trails
or touch receipts for any other command action. The remaining digest, command id
and operator references are still classified as linkable evidence, not anonymous data. These periods and bases await the
same counsel review as the other proposed classes; no new processor is added.

## Retained after erasure

Erasure closes the account. It deletes everything in the classes marked
"Deleted" and queues vendor deletion, in one transaction. It keeps:

- the competitive record, including the username, which stays reserved by
  the unique `lower(username)` index and is shown as closed. Visibility is
  unchanged: a closed member's profile shows only what any unrelated viewer
  could see before (public Rounds only);
- the `users` row id and the `actors` link to it, which the record needs;
- the keyed email digests on delivery receipts and suppressions;
- ranked content, and the agreement signatures that stand behind it.

Spectator chat is not ranked content, on any Round: erasure scrubs the member's
messages in place, keeping only each message's place in the channel.

Erasure asks for every casual Recording the member sits in to go, whole
(`recordingPrivacyAdopter`): the row records the deletion intent in the erasure's
own transaction and the deletion sweep (`apps/web/src/server/recording-deletions.ts`)
removes the objects, retrying until storage confirms. The row then stays as a
tombstone holding its Round, class, why it went, its eligibility (for a casual one, the
opting-in host's actor id) and its capture row's timings and Egress ids, with no media and no
keys. Moderation may take any Recording down, and may then remove it; only a legal ground
removes one still up.

**Evidence retention.** A casual Recording with an open report on it or its Round survives
erasure and a participant's removal, hidden from everyone and not restorable, until the report
closes or 90 days pass, and is then deleted the same way. A moderation removal is refused while
the report is open, and a failed capture's cleanup waits. Only an operator's legal removal,
with a closed legal ground on the audit record, deletes it sooner. Its purpose is to keep what a
report rests on until the report is decided; the period and basis are **not reviewed by
counsel**. With no open report it goes at once.

A Recording's source video waits in the private
hold bucket until it is built and nothing else reads it; a removal also clears that, and
is not `removed` until it has.

How long private seconds can live in the hold bucket: revocation keeps them out of it
where it lands in time, but a late revocation, or an unexpected phase, can put some there.
Nothing published ever holds them (ADR 0099). **Not reviewed by counsel.**

| Case                                     | How long the hold lasts                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A Broadcast with no Recording            | Until its feed is withdrawn: 60 s after the Broadcast completes, or at a stop once the Egress is gone.                                                               |
| A Recording that builds                  | Until it is `ready` and the Broadcast or capture no longer reads it. Normally minutes after the Round, plus the build.                                               |
| A build that keeps failing               | Until five runs have failed and nothing else reads the source. It is then stripped to whole segments history proves public, and anything unknown then goes for good. |
| Failed and stripped                      | Only known-public seconds, kept for a rebuild until removal: no retention limit if ranked, seven days if casual.                                                     |
| A Recording taken down                   | Kept as it stood, private seconds included, as evidence, until the report closes or 90 days pass.                                                                    |
| A Round the web tier never returned from | Until an operator acts: nothing runs to clear it.                                                                                                                    |

Ranked content is its own class, not part of the competitive record. Its stated
purpose (owner, 2026-11-02) is that ranked season Rounds are qualifying artifacts
for tournaments, so results and video stand as the evidence behind qualification,
disputes and integrity review. A participant cannot delete it, account erasure
included; moderation can still take it down. The class, its purpose, its basis and
its indefinite period are **proposed and not yet reviewed by counsel** (COMP-4),
and the policy may change after that review. The waiver the class rests on is a
draft for counsel ([ranked content waiver](../legal/ranked-content-waiver.md)).
Nothing here states that it has been legally reviewed or approved.

A ranked Round's transcript lines belong to this class, so the 180-day window does not
reach them. Erasure keeps them, along
with the Round's pending judgment. A casual Round's lines still erase with the member.

Erasure deletes the member's own transcript lines from every non-ranked Round, and
discards every held AI judgment of such a Round they sat in (DEC-223). It also clears every Round analysis of it, the other debater's
too. Round analysis purging removes report content and all future dispatch or
publication authority while retaining the original competitive seat identity and
bounded execution observations (historical claim/token facts, request time,
fixed deadline, call model, token counts and known/unknown outcomes). These facts
prevent a privacy/content purge from silently resetting usage or inventing a
provider refund. No transcript, prompt, raw answer or payment credential remains
in that metadata; exports omit attempt authenticators. The account's separate
allowance ledger is erased under its own adopter. Late accounting cannot recreate
it or authorize another request. Analysis content retention is 180 days from the
actual request, or original creation for a historical report; it is not a
Round-age eligibility window. Current all-participant privacy fences still refuse
requests and private report reads after erasure. Limits of that:

- Other people's lines stay until their 180-day retention ends, and can repeat
  the member's words: a bot's reply, an answer in cross-examination.
- A result already decided, its ballots and its rating changes stay as the
  competitive record, and a submitted ballot's reason and feedback can quote the
  member. A held judgment discarded leaves its Round with no ruling from that
  judge, so erasing can void a result not yet decided (ISSUE-1170).
- A Round still running, a capture in flight or a judging job not yet answered
  can write after the erasure (ISSUE-1169).

A username is personal data (ICO guidance on identifiers), so keeping it is a
decision under a lawful basis, not a claim that it is anonymous. The proposed
basis is legitimate interest in an accurate competitive record that other
members' results and ratings depend on. A member may object (Art. 21); the
owner decides each objection with counsel, and an upheld objection replaces
the username on the closed account by hand. This exception, the basis and the
indefinite period are proposed pending COMP-4.

Future alt and ban-evasion detection reads the retained row id, actor, username
and email digests. It is a separate future capability with its own
legitimate-interest assessment and retention; nothing is retained for it
today beyond what the record and deliverability already keep.

## Children

Members must be 16 or older (DEC-244, one trusted
configuration value). The age step stores only a birth month. An under-13
answer is refused neutrally: nothing is stored and the provisional account is
deleted at once, with its sign-in links and delivery receipts (WAIT-4.7).
Daisy is not directed to children under 13. A later under-age answer against
a recorded adult age is open (DEC-246).

## Necessary transcription recovery evidence (DEC-369)

This class is separate from optional Recordings and ranked content. Its purpose
is finishing and quality-reconciling authoritative transcription before the single immutable judging freeze, including recovery after platform failure. The authorized
local implementation direction is encrypted admitted PCM plus original-authority
provenance, with a hard maximum seven days from the ORIGINAL trusted receipt and
no retry extension. Delete earlier only after durably authoritative selected
transcript, adequate actual coverage AND explicit recognition settlement under the
declared policy, or applicable erasure. Provider success alone
is insufficient. Current production transcription still stores no recovery audio;
production collection remains disabled pending REL-H infrastructure/privacy proof
and human sign-off. Synthetic isolated qualification is not production collection.

Provisional captions and unselected recognition candidates are separate private
working text, linked to original receipts/speaker/sequence/time and attempt/model/
configuration/version and reconciliation decisions. They are not final judging
evidence and never prove coverage or recognition completeness. Their candidate
lifecycle is minimized with the original evidence: the original seven-day receipt
cap cannot be restarted by recognition or caption revision. Purge unnecessary
candidate text on settlement/erasure, retaining only the selected authoritative
transcript under its separate transcript policy and the minimal declared decision
provenance. No candidate text or audio belongs in telemetry or ordinary realtime
payloads. This candidate lifecycle is planned and production-disabled; implementation,
export/erasure/restore and every-copy proof remain required before activation.

PostgreSQL intent routing additionally retains the original bounded subject actor
IDs, separately from the current cast or successor worker. These are linkable
personal/private privacy-routing metadata, not anonymous identifiers. They permit
immediate canonical subject fencing and bounded cleanup after aggregate erasure.
Do not expose them through ordinary Round reads, worker discovery or telemetry;
minimize them after confirmed retirement. Original capture and current execution
worker identifiers remain separate private routing facts. The minimal sticky
Round withdrawal marker follows its owning Round lifecycle; it contains no PCM,
transcript or subject identities. Unknown original subjects cannot be
backfilled from current membership or treated as an empty set. Integrated
retirement/export/erasure remains incomplete and production-disabled.

Private per-intent enrollment offer rows retain one original evidence ID and
actual last-offered timestamp, without audio, transcript or key material. This is
linkable traversal metadata, not evidence acceptance/current authority. It
cascades with the owning intent; the same minimization/export/restore obligations
apply, without a new permanent retention purpose or production approval.

The private enrollment scan position retains at most two linkable Round IDs,
ordering timestamps and a next-due instant, without payload/subjects/tokens. A
completed or empty finite pass clears its cursor references. This bounded control
row has no authority over an original receipt, custody erasure or transcript.
An incomplete pass can survive restart/aggregate deletion; metadata minimization,
export and restore obligations apply below, without production retention approval.

Private transcription-recovery attempt metadata additionally retains original
context/claim epoch, conservative call ordinal, pinned model/policy, outcome,
candidate digest/byte count and admission/completion timestamps. These linkable
private facts contain no candidate words and do not establish recognition quality.
An unknown admission never creates another permission under the same epoch.
Attempts cascade with their owning context on aggregate erasure and share the
context metadata minimization/export/restore obligation below; no new permanent
metadata retention or production privacy approval is asserted.

Private transcription-recovery contexts and source manifests retain receipt IDs,
original stream/grant/run digests, offsets/sequences, bounded context membership
and work/selection provenance. These are linkable private metadata, not anonymous
facts or permission to dispatch. They contain no PCM or unselected candidate text.
The frozen original subject/speaker/custody scope participates in the context key;
a successor cast never supplies missing privacy authority. Unselected candidate
text remains transient; loss requires rerecognition from original evidence, never
reconstruction from a digest. Minimize unnecessary context/source metadata after
confirmed lifecycle completion, retaining only declared selected-transcript or
exception/remedy provenance under its owning policy. Round aggregate deletion
removes context metadata while original custody-cleanup routing survives in the
evidence-owned intent. Full export/erasure/restore/metadata-minimization and
production retention acceptance remain incomplete under REL-H.

The independent local key authority additionally retains canonical original
Round/subject scope bindings and accepted privacy-intent routing (exact actor/user
or Round, opaque intent ID, authorization digest, original sequence and sticky
pending/committed state). These are private/linkable necessary metadata, never
authentication by themselves or evidence that restored PG is current. Pending
refuses raw use without permitting uncertain material deletion. Committed denial
remains effective against restored old material while current authority is
available. Accepted privacy work must resume the same request after a crash; no
missing restored event cancels it. Canonical erasure must still commit during
custody/vendor outage. Actual public-closure integration, scheduled recovery,
export and restore-lineage reconciliation are incomplete; no production capability
is enabled.

The key authority is separate from payload backups. Its opaque evidence IDs,
original receipt/expiry, integrity digests and revocation tombstones are necessary
anti-resurrection metadata; they contain no PCM, transcript or raw key. Retain the
minimal fence while any old payload/material snapshot may be restored. Purging it
requires verified retirement of all such copies or an independently irreversible
replacement fence; no automatic seven-day metadata purge may re-enable old keys.
The human privacy/infrastructure owner must approve the precise metadata retention
and retirement evidence before activation. Local synthetic tests remove their
isolated authority/material directories at cleanup.

Private application recovery decodes necessary evidence only after original
subject/withdrawal/frontier/expiry fences and current independent custody checks.
Its original body stays private to the owning recovery job, never ordinary Round
reads, realtime, telemetry or read-only worker discovery. Decoding is not new
retention, a new processor or permission to dispatch: the consuming job must bound
its transient plaintext, honor the ORIGINAL expiry and current erasure, cancel and
clean up, and take its own current dispatch/commit fences. An already copied
plaintext cannot be cryptographically recalled. Local synthetic tests demonstrate
receipt/body recovery and future-use refusal, not every-copy deletion, public
closure, full PG backup restoration or external-processor cancellation. Production
remains disabled pending REL-H; capture/STT/job-lifecycle integration is incomplete.

Encrypted backup bytes are not physically deleted by SQL DELETE or key revocation.
Every-copy expiry, at-rest encryption, access, restore reconciliation and permanent
key revocation remain unverified production obligations in REL-H. Rolling back all
local authority copies has no local anti-rollback proof. Already dispatched
plaintext cannot be recalled cryptographically: cancellation, future dispatch and
commit fences, temporary-data cleanup and processor erasure must be demonstrated
separately. No logs, ordinary Round reads or realtime output may expose this class.

## Security measures

Zero trust at every boundary with validated input; passwordless sign-in
(magic links and passkeys, [ADR 0025](../decisions/0025-auth-delivery-and-abuse-protection.md));
rate limits on authentication and mutations; SHA3-256 digests for stored
secrets and recipient keys ([ADR 0019](../decisions/0019-token-secret-ownership.md));
unguessable cuid2 identifiers; least-privilege database roles; a separate
admin app, database and operator identity ([ADR 0043](../decisions/0043-no-admin-surface-in-participant-app.md),
[ADR 0077](../decisions/0077-admin-app-architecture.md)); structured logs
without personal data; security views over the operational log store.

## Breaches

A suspected personal-data breach is posted on the PageSpace Incidents channel
at once, then recorded there with what happened, the data and people
affected, the likely consequences and the remediation (GDPR Art. 33(5)
requires a record of every breach, notified or not). The owner decides with
counsel whether to notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours of
awareness (Art. 33), whether to tell the people affected (Art. 34), and which
US state notification laws apply. Detection uses the admin app's security
views over the operational log store.

## Product analytics data lifecycle

Optional product analytics uses consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a), Art. 7 and ePrivacy/PECR). `consent_record` stores the account identifier, contract version, necessary/analytics/replay choices and last-choice timestamp. It is private account data retained until account erasure and included in subject export. The necessary HttpOnly consent cookie expires after 180 days; same-origin local storage holds only the latest consent/identity synchronization marker, replaced on withdrawal/account change. SDK identity cookies exist only after opt-in and are cleared on withdrawal or identity reset. Vendor events contain bounded product facts and scoped identifiers, never participant content. The owner activated PostHog project 699106 (US Cloud, Free plan, 1M events/month) on 2026-11-07 after DPA signature and live publication of privacy draft-0.3; the signed agreement includes DPF and applicable standard contractual clauses, and the project showed organization-default one-year event retention; person/event/recording erasure uses durable vendor jobs. Session replay is disabled for this beta. See [product analytics](product-analytics.md) for configuration and proof.

Identified analytics withdrawal atomically records denied consent and rearms a bounded PostHog vendor-deletion intent. Repeated requests remain one row per subject/vendor; a new deletion generation replaces its job ID so an older delivery cannot acknowledge a newer request. Account erasure rearms the same delivery obligation after any earlier withdrawal. Anonymous withdrawal stops future capture and removes the browser identity cookie; pre-existing anonymous events expire under the selected one-year vendor retention period. Expiry is not physical deletion: PostHog may retain expired events longer and deletes in batches; no fixed physical-deletion deadline has been confirmed ([vendor documentation](https://posthog.com/docs/data/events-retention)). The owner-authorized activation retains this anonymous history behavior; withdrawal does not retroactively delete an unlinked anonymous event. Direct live proof covers anonymous page views, withdrawal/reload silence and a synthetic absent-subject deletion acknowledgement, not the full signed-in/linked-person erasure journey. See the dated receipt and proof limits in [product analytics](product-analytics.md). Global PRIV-H remains open for other processors and obligations.

### Beta invitation and preapproval data

Beta preapprovals store normalized recipient emails to admit invited beta
participants before signup. Unused grants remain until an owner removes them;
claimed grants remain until removal or account erasure. Account export includes
matching and claimed grants. Erasure removes both, plus their invitation
intents, before clearing the account email. Removing a claimed grant does not
revoke the account. Audit events and command receipts contain opaque ids and
closed outcomes, never emails. The admin database holds the email in an
unresolved typed pending command so a lost response can be reconciled; it
removes that pending row on settlement or abandonment. This data never enters
URLs, logs, analytics or error text. No new processor is added: invitations use
the existing suppression-aware mail transport. Terminal invitation intents
follow the existing mail-intent retention; expired delivery detail is displayed
as unknown. Unsent mail is cancelled when the grant is removed or the account
is erased, revoked or restricted; a provider request in flight cannot be
recalled. Invitation URLs carry no credential and confer no authority.
